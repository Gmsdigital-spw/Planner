WHAT A GOOD EPISODE IS (Grayson's feedback, Oct 19 2026)
A real STORY, not a summary. First person, or a narrator telling what a character did: specific details (names, numbers, days, what someone said), a clear setup with stakes, two or three escalations, a twist, and a final button line. 11-14 short lines, 38-52 seconds. Each line is one picture: one clear scene the image AI can draw. Example that works (EP 11): "I owe a six-year-old forty dollars, and it's completely my fault. My little brother Max is six, and he's been begging to play my game for a month. I kept telling him no. It's too hard. There's a boss I've been stuck on for a week. Forty-seven tries... I counted. Saturday he caught me rage quitting. And he goes, can I just try one time? So I handed him the controller. Mostly to watch him lose. [30 SECONDS LATER] He... didn't lose. He didn't even take damage. He put the controller down, looked at me, and said... that one's easy. Then he went back to his juice box like nothing happened. [ONE WEEK LATER] Now every time I get stuck, he charges me five dollars a boss. And this morning he raised his prices. Which is why..." Not this: "my brother won a game, now he charges me $5" (too thin). No lists of fees, no price-tag dumps, no AI-slop phrasing, nothing that needs on-screen gameplay to make sense.
